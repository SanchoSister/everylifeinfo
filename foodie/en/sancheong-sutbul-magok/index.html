🥩Seoul Foodie: Sancheong Sutbul Garden Magok — Jirisan Black Pork BBQ

A real-visit review (visited 2026.07.23) of one of Magok's most talked-about BBQ restaurants — usually famous for brutal waits, but we got lucky.

About this place

  • Sancheong Sutbul Garden is a Jirisan black pork charcoal BBQ specialist run by the owner behind Mongtan, Gyodae 2-Story House, and Godosik — well-known Seoul restaurants. This Magok branch is nearly as busy as the original Euljiro location, near Balsan Station.
  • We arrived just past the weekday lunch peak and got seated immediately — normally this place has a notoriously long wait, so timing your visit outside peak hours is worth it.
  • The interior has a retro 80s–90s feel with stone-slab tables, spacious enough for a lively, buzzing atmosphere during meal times. It's also known as a baby- and pregnancy-friendly restaurant, with a "pregnant fast pass" and baby rice available on the menu.

What we ordered

  • Traditional salt-grilled pork (재래식 소금구이) — one full plate, plus a half plate as a refill
  • Jirisan arrowroot cold noodles, plain style (지리산 칡냉면 물냉면)
  • Shijimi-clam doenjang rice with makgeolli-style broth (재첩된장술밥)
  • Side dishes included seasoned perilla leaves, chive salad, and egg salad — all well matched to the meat.
  • Note: exact menu prices weren't listed for us, but the overall price point runs on the higher side for Korean BBQ.

The food

  • Salt-grilled pork: Jirisan black pork belly and pork neck (오겹살+목살), grilled together with scallions and mushrooms. Staff grilled the first plate for us start to finish, handling the heat and flipping timing — very convenient. Portions were cut generously large, so we cut them smaller ourselves a few times. Sauces included salt, wasabi, pollack roe ssamjang, and mud snail sauce — staff recommended trying salt first. That first bite dipped in salt was the standout moment: the black pork's rich meaty aroma came through with the flavor of the meat itself intact.
  • The refill half plate was self-grilled and just as good, especially paired with the chive salad.
  • Shijimi-clam doenjang rice: a lighter, clearer doenjang broth thanks to the shijimi clams — savory but not too salty, felt like exactly what you need after a heavy meat meal.
  • Arrowroot naengmyeon: the noodles have a distinctly dark color and a chewier bite than regular naengmyeon. The signature move here is a generous pile of thinly julienned cucumber (staff will warn you in advance if you're not a cucumber fan). The broth had restrained, natural umami rather than artificial sweetness — a refreshing way to close out the meal.
  • A milk-flavored ice cream was served after checkout, a nice way to cut through the richness of the meal.

Good to know

  • Go right after the weekday lunch peak to skip the notoriously long wait.
  • Family-friendly: baby rice on the menu, plus a "pregnant fast pass" for expecting visitors.
  • CatchTable supports remote waitlist registration if you want to line up before arriving.

Overall

  • Prices run a bit high, but nothing here felt like a waste. Between the quality of the Jirisan black pork, the staff grilling it for you, the retro-hip space, and the naengmyeon and doenjang rice sides, it felt more like a full experience than just a meal. Worth returning to for a special meal in the Magok area.
